How to read a tide table

A tide table is a forecast. It gives you four numbers a day, and almost everything you need on the water can be worked out from those four.

What the table shows

Each line lists a time and a height. The height is measured from chart datum, the level the sea rarely falls below, so a height of 0.4 m means 0.4 m of water above the depths printed on your chart. Most coasts get two high waters and two low waters every 24 hours and 50 minutes.

That extra 50 minutes is why the tide comes later each day. If low water is at 06:12 today, expect it near 07:00 tomorrow. Your harbour office prints the local corrections.

Reading the four columns

  1. Find today’s date, then the next high water after you plan to leave.
  2. Subtract the low-water height from the high-water height to get the range.
  3. Add your boat’s draught and a safety margin of at least 0.5 m.

Springs and neaps

Twice a month, around the new and the full moon, the range is at its largest. These are spring tides. A week later come the neaps, when high water is lower and low water is higher. At Solvik quay the range runs from about 0.6 m at neaps to 1.3 m at springs.
