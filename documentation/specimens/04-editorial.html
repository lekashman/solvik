The bay that keeps the sun

Four hours of winter light, a ferry twice a day, and a harbour wall built from round stones.
Words and photographs by Ingrid Aasen · 9 minute read

Solvik means sun bay, and the name is a plain description. The hills to the north and east stop the wind, and the mouth of the bay faces south-west, so the low winter sun reaches the quay from eleven until three.

The first ferry leaves at 08:15. By then the bakery has sold 60 loaves and the school boat is back from the outer islands.

“You can tell the month by which window the light comes through.”

Most of the 340 people who live here work on the water or for someone who does. The harbour wall dates from 1894, and each stone in it was rolled smooth by the sea before anyone lifted it.

In June the light does not leave at all. The ferry keeps its timetable, and the gulls keep theirs.
