Solvik is a sans-serif family in seven weights, from Extra Light to Black, each with an italic. It is derived from Inter by Rasmus Andersson and moves it toward the geometric end of the grotesque genre. Andrew Lekashman made it for presentations and slide decks, where a few words at large sizes have to carry the emphasis and the same family still has to set the labels, tables and footnotes around them. To contribute, see github.com/lekashman/solvik.

The round letters are the clearest difference from Inter. They are widened until the o is close to a circle in every weight, while the stroke weight stays the same, so the bowls open up without the text getting darker. The terminals are cut flat. In the italics, a, e and f are the upright letters slanted, not Inter’s cursive forms, so an italic word keeps the same skeleton as the roman word next to it and reads as the same voice with a lean.

Emphasis on a slide usually comes from weight, so the heavy end of the family got the most attention. In SemiBold and heavier, the horizontal strokes are held closer to the verticals in thickness, which keeps Bold, Extra Bold and Black even in colour at title sizes. The seven weights are spaced so that pairs two or three steps apart contrast clearly: a Black headline over a Light subtitle, an Extra Light numeral beside a SemiBold label, a Bold phrase inside a Regular sentence.

Numbers are often the largest thing on a slide. Solvik’s figures are about 8% wider than Inter’s in Regular, and the straight-sided letters are about 2% wider. Both differences shrink toward Black, where they are about 1%. Tabular figures are available through the tnum feature for tables and financial statements, and the alternates inherited from Inter remain available through their OpenType features.

The spacing and kerning were rebuilt for the new shapes. Among the 331 Latin letters, figures and punctuation marks that were checked (Basic Latin, Latin-1, Latin Extended-A and common punctuation), no two sit closer than 8% of the stem width in any of the 14 styles, so wide accents and heavy diagonals do not touch their neighbours in a large title. The exceptions are pairs that join by design: ff, ft, tt and runs of dashes or underscores.

Solvik covers Latin (including Vietnamese), Cyrillic and Greek, with the same character set as Inter plus the combining double acute accent. Google Fonts’ shaperglot tool, version 1.2.2, reports 792 languages as supported in every style: 723 written in Latin script, 68 in Cyrillic, and Greek. Combining marks are positioned over Cyrillic and Greek letters as well as Latin ones, so stress accents work in Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, Bulgarian and Serbian text.

The family is published as 14 static fonts built from UFO sources. The repository includes specimen pages that show Solvik in an interface, a magazine layout, a long article, a web page, a set of wordmarks and a slide deck, along with the scripts that document how the sources were derived from Inter.
